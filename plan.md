# Applied AI Career Research and Content Plan

## Purpose, Scope, and Research Standard

This document is a **content and career-positioning plan**, not a portfolio UI or visual-design plan. It consolidates the August 15, 2026 research on comparable Applied AI roles at AI startups and large technology companies, public-profile patterns, and the required improvements to Nikunj Khitha’s resume, LinkedIn, portfolio copy, and public AI narrative.

It deliberately excludes recommendations about layout, component design, animations, color, navigation, or other portfolio UI changes. Future implementation work should update content only after the supporting evidence for every material claim has been reviewed.

### Evidence labels used in this plan

- **Confirmed personal evidence** — a contribution, boundary, or result supplied by Nikunj and/or captured in approved internal source material. It is still subject to confidentiality review before publication.
- **Claim needs documentation before publication** — a number or outcome that may be true but lacks a public-safe baseline, date, methodology, or other context needed to make it defensible.
- **Current job-level source** — a specific public job posting retrieved on August 15, 2026. Openings and requirements can change; recheck immediately before applying.
- **Role-family source** — an official careers page or current careers board that establishes a hiring category but does not prove the requirements or availability of a particular opening.
- **Dynamic/unverified at research time** — a title or page was observed but the underlying requirements or active status could not be reliably extracted. Treat it as market nomenclature only.
- **Near-term target / selective stretch / future benchmark** — a candid fit classification, not a statement of eligibility or a prediction of hiring outcome.

### Public-safety baseline

Do not publish or imply any information that exposes AWS accounts, buckets, data topology, private hosts, internal URLs, Jira IDs, customer names, employee data, proprietary workflows, credentials, or raw ticket text. Public naming permission for a system does not make its operational implementation public.

The supplied BI architecture diagram is private evidence only. Do not publish it, recreate it, or describe its topology. Credentials identified in local ticket exports should be treated as potentially compromised and handled through a separate rotation/revocation process; ticket exports must be redacted before they are committed, shared, archived, or uploaded.

## Hiring-Market Research: What Comparable Roles Actually Ask For

### Cross-market pattern

Across current AI startup, platform, and large-tech roles, the common requirement is not simply familiarity with a model API. Strong candidates are expected to:

1. Turn ambiguous operational, product, or customer problems into scoped AI systems.
2. Build and operate agents, tool-calling workflows, retrieval/context layers, and application backends.
3. Evaluate quality, safety, behavior, and reliability rather than treating a demo as completion.
4. Integrate with existing data, permissions, business processes, and user workflows.
5. Turn a successful implementation into a reusable pattern, asset, playbook, or platform capability.
6. Communicate with engineering, product, operators, executives, and sometimes customers.
7. Measure adoption, reliability, delivery quality, productivity, or operational outcomes when the evidence is available.

Nikunj’s current narrative already has strong raw material for points 1–5. The main content gap is making the operational outcome, evaluation evidence, and ownership boundary easy to understand without a recruiter parsing dense tool lists.

### Patterns across credible Applied AI, agent, product, and FDE profiles

1. **A clear functional identity beats a buzzword stack.** Effective profiles usually say role + audience, role + product surface, role + system domain, or delivery role + environment.
2. **A career through-line creates trust.** Developer platforms → AI developer experience; applied science → frontier Applied AI; enterprise delivery → FDE → Applied AI; search/context → enterprise agents.
3. **Public artifacts beat self-description.** Repositories, live products, technical posts, talks, papers, benchmark notes, and bounded case studies are stronger than adjectives such as “enterprise-grade” or “scalable.”
4. **Operational evidence differentiates AI engineers.** Evaluation, permissions, deployment, failure handling, observability, feedback loops, human review, and rollout evidence separate durable systems from demos.
5. **Methods make metrics credible.** A test set, success definition, permission matrix, regression gate, read-back check, benchmark environment, or known limitation gives a number meaning.
6. **Technical writing is a career asset.** Strong profiles publish reasoning: why a design was chosen, which failure mode mattered, what was measured, and what limitation remained.
7. **Scope discipline increases credibility.** Clear boundaries among personal contribution, shared work, maturity, and omitted confidential detail make strong claims more believable.
8. **Senior profiles are clarity models, not equivalence claims.** Nikunj should borrow narrative discipline and proof habits, not titles, tenure, customer access, organizational leadership, or research status.
